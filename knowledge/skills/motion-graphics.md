# Skill: motion-graphics reels (Hassan)
Weblume's approved reels (weblume-ad: Day, Kinetic, Newspaper, Never Closed, Cafe) teach the rules:
- Second 0-1 decides everything: open with a "hook" or "kinetic" scene that states the pain or the promise in 2-5
  words, big. Never open on the logo.
- One idea per scene, 2-4 seconds each, 15-30 seconds in total. Change something on screen at least every 1.5 s.
- Kinetic type is the backbone: few words, huge, one after another ("kinetic"), the last word in red; Arabic words
  whole (never letter by letter).
- Show the real work: the client's site scrolling in a phone ("site"), the real menu, real photos. On a reel about a
  client's site, "image" scenes use that site's own photos (the code picks them); AI photos only for mood when the
  site has none, never to fake a product (no cupcakes for a shop that sells samosa and maamoul).
- Kinetic lines: 3-5 short words. Each word needs half a second on screen and the first one waits for the red wipe,
  so a kinetic scene lasts at least 0.8 s + 0.5 s per word (the code stretches it if you give less).
- Showing the same site twice? The second phone scene opens a different page; give it a caption about a different
  benefit, or use one longer site scene instead.
- Sound: the code lays the music bed of Weblume's approved kinetic reel under every reel and fades it out at the end.
- Numbers only when true ("counter": 3 days to launch, 0 SAR before the design). Never invented sales or clients.
- Rhythm: hook → problem → the site (proof) → one benefit as a counter or kinetic line → the brand reveal ("logo")
  → the call to action (added by the code: the Weblume website, never a phone number).
- Every scene enters with motion; nothing sits still for a whole scene. Choose each scene's entrance ("in") by what
  the cut means, like an editor:
  - "zoom" (flies in from the lens): the hook's answer, a big reveal, the first look at the client's site.
  - "slide" (whip pan): "and then", moving on to the next benefit; fast, so use it between two quick beats.
  - "iris" (opens from a red circle): a reveal that echoes a round shape: the logo ring, a plate, a cup, a number.
  - "shutter" (red bars open): a new chapter: problem → solution, or before the brand reveal.
  - "glitch" (a red jolt): the pain point or "the old way is broken"; at most once per reel, it's loud.
  - "wipe" (red slash): the house cut; safe anywhere.
  - "flip" (turns over like a card): "the other side": before/after, paper menu → digital menu.
  - "split" (opens from a line in the middle): a clean, calm reveal of a site or a product.
  - "rise" (swiped up like a story): the natural move into a phone screen or the next item in a list.
- Scene "photos": the client's real products as three cards fanning out; the best proof after the site itself.
- The idea comes first. A good reel has ONE idea a viewer can retell in a sentence, told as a tiny story:
  a moment the viewer knows (the WhatsApp message asking "عندكم إيه النهارده؟", the paper menu with crossed-out prices,
  the customer waiting while you text the photo) → the turn (the site does it) → the proof (the real site, the real
  products) → the brand. Don't list features; show the moment. Each new reel tries a different idea and opening.
- After the first cut the code shows you frames of your reel and you fix what a viewer would notice (cut text, weak
  photo, repeated look, boring stretch); then it is filmed again.
  Never the same entrance twice in a row; 3-4 different ones per reel feel edited, 6 different ones feel random.
  Motivate the cut: let the shape or direction carry over (a red circle into the logo ring, a slide that follows the
  phone's scroll). Hard cuts land on the beat of the music; slow push-ins hold the calm lines.
- Hierarchy on every frame: one thing to read, big, centred on the safe area (no text in the top 10% or bottom 20%,
  where the app's buttons sit). The brand red marks only the one word or number that matters.
- Quality: the owner picks High / Medium / Fast in the dashboard. High is 60 fps and the sharpest encode; it's slow,
  and he prefers it.
- Look: black, red, white (Weblume), or the client's colours when the reel is about the client's site.
- Scene types: hook, text, kinetic, counter, site (phone/laptop), image, logo; the code adds the cta.
Reference skills on this laptop for bigger productions: motion-reel (beat grids, shot lists, critique on contact
sheets) and remotion (React video). Hassan's player draws the reel itself, frame by frame, no paid tools.

## Copying a reel the owner sends
When the owner uploads a video with a video request, it is a style reference. The code reads it (colours, font,
weight, photo framing, how text moves, its entrances, seconds per shot) and draws the whole reel in that look; you
match its rhythm: scene lengths near its shot length, its kind of text scenes, its entrances first. Copy the style,
never its words, logos, footage or music. The content is still the request's, and the Weblume logo reveal and end
card stay black, red and white: that is how every copied reel still ends as a Weblume reel.
